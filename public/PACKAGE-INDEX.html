The Vision

The world's largest classroom

One building. Thirteen floors. Every surface a screen.

The Chet Holifield Federal Building is a one-million-square-foot stepped pyramid in Southern California. Built in 1971. Vacant since 2025. Nobody knows what to do with it.

We want to wrap it in displays and turn it into the world's most visible educational institution. Not a building that houses education. A building that IS education.

☀️ Day
Visualizations of learning happening worldwide. Real-time data made visible.
🌙 Night
Dark mode. Respectful to neighbors. The building sleeps.

Why Screens?

The building is not beautiful. It never was. It was built for Rockwell to manufacture aerospace equipment. They never moved in. It's been called "one part government building, two parts apocalyptic stronghold."

Preservation means maintaining something worth preserving as it is. This building's value is its form — the stepped pyramid, the scale, the audacity. Not the beige concrete panels.

Covering it in screens preserves the form. The silhouette stays. The terraces stay. What changes is the skin — from dead concrete to living light.

The Sphere in Las Vegas cost $2.3 billion and exists to sell concert tickets. We propose the same technology in service of education.

Stakeholder Analysis

Know the players

🏛️ GSA (General Services Administration) Gatekeeper

What they want: Get rid of this problem. Not look bad. Follow procedure.

What they fear: Another failed auction. More lawsuits. Congressional scrutiny.

Your pitch: "You've tried auctions. They keep failing. We're offering a clean educational use that ends the saga."

🏥 Hoag Memorial Hospital Competitor

What they are: Largest health system in Orange County. $1.7B revenue. Second bidder in last auction.

What they want: South County expansion. The land more than the building. Probably demolition.

Your advantage: You're not competing on use case. They've had two chances and both fell through.

🏗️ Hilco Development / Pintar Investment Competitor

What they are: Developers. Won original auction at $177M. Deal cancelled after Hoag lawsuit.

What they want: Demolition. Mixed-use development.

Your advantage: Legal baggage. GSA cancelled their deal. Their plan requires demolition, which is controversial.

🏙️ City of Laguna Niguel Gatekeeper

Power: Controls zoning AFTER federal sale. Cannot block federal transaction.

What they want: Appropriate development. Tax revenue. Happy residents.

Your pitch (later): "This isn't housing. It's an educational institution. Positive attention. Field trips. Jobs without massive traffic."

Timing: Don't engage until after GSA deal is locked.

🏛️ Preserve Orange County / National Trust Potential Ally

What they want: Building preserved. Adaptive reuse. No demolition.

Your pitch: "The structure stays. The terraces stay. Would you rather have screens or a pile of rubble?"

Timing: After GSA deal is progressing. They could be helpful advocates.

🏠 Neighbors Unknown

What they want: No disruption. No traffic. No light pollution. Ideally, for the building to disappear.

What they'll do: Can pressure city council. Can make life difficult with opposition.

Strategy: Stay invisible until after acquisition. Then engage with specific community benefits. Night mode. Field trips. Emphasize what you're NOT.

Acquisition Pathways

How to get the building

💡
Recommended Path: Lead with Public Benefit Conveyance, fall back to Negotiated Hybrid. You can't outbid Hoag in an auction.
Path A: Competitive Auction

What: GSA runs another public auction. You bid.

Pros: Clear rules, straightforward process.

Cons: Hoag has $1.7B revenue. You don't.

Your odds: Low

Path B: Negotiated Sale

What: GSA negotiates directly when auction has failed (it has, three times).

Pros: No bidding war. GSA has discretion.

Cons: Still need to demonstrate financial capacity.

Your odds: Medium

Path C: Public Benefit Conveyance ⭐

What: GSA transfers at no/reduced cost for educational use.

Pros: Could get building for free. Educational use explicitly allowed.

Cons: Typically requires 501(c)(3). Need nonprofit partner.

Your odds: Medium-High

Path D: Hybrid

What: Below-market sale with public benefit terms.

Pros: Flexibility. GSA gets something.

Cons: Requires GSA to be creative.

Your odds: Unknown
