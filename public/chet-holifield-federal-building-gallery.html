The Chet Holifield Federal Building

A Historical Visual Record of California's Iconic Ziggurat — From Construction (1968-1971) to Present Day

Laguna Niguel, California

Building Overview

One of America's most distinctive federal buildings, designed by William L. Pereira & Associates

Architect

William L. Pereira & Associates — known for San Francisco's Transamerica Pyramid and LAX Theme Building

Construction

1968–1971 — Originally built for Rockwell International's Autonetics headquarters

Size

Over 1,000,000 sq ft on 92 acres — with 6,200 parking spaces radiating diagonally

Style

Late Modern / Brutalist — Seven-tiered stepped pyramid resembling ancient Mesopotamian ziggurats

Materials

Angled pre-cast concrete panels with reticulation (textured curvilinear finish)

Location

24000 Avila Road, Laguna Niguel, CA 92677 — 4 miles from Pacific coastline

Historical Timeline

Key moments in the building's history

1968
Construction begins for North American Rockwell's Autonetics division headquarters
1971
Construction completed — Rockwell never occupies after defense contract falls through
1974
GSA assumes ownership — traded for three surplus government facilities
1978
Rededicated as Chet Holifield Federal Building (honoring CA-19 Congressman)
2003
Energy-efficient roofing upgrade saves $650,000/year in utility costs
2020
GSA begins planning to transfer building out of federal ownership
2024-2025
Property auctioned — winning bid of $177 million, sale contested

Architectural Significance

Character-defining features as documented by GSA

Stepped Pyramidal Form

Seven tiers referencing ancient Mesopotamian ziggurats — one of only two modern ziggurat buildings on the National Register

Below-Grade Construction

Majority of the 1M+ sq ft is built below grade, diminishing visual mass while maximizing usable space

Pre-Cast Concrete Panels

Angled panels with reticulated (textured curvilinear) finish that captures light and shadow

Decorative Moat

Smooth rock moat surrounding three sides, creating a "modern fortress" effect

Efficient Circulation

Pereira's design allows maximum 5-minute travel between any two points in the building

Symmetrical Plaza

Classical-inspired formal entrance with grass panels, trees, and 2,500+ original landscaping trees
