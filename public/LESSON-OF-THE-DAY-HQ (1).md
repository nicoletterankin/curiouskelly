# LESSON OF THE DAY

**Global Headquarters Proposal**

---

## The Building Nobody Loves

The Chet Holifield Federal Building sits on 92 acres in Laguna Niguel, California. One million square feet. Thirteen stepped floors. Built in 1971. Vacant since 2025.

Everyone agrees it's ugly.

Developers want to tear it down and build housing. Preservationists want to save it because William Pereira designed it. Neighbors want it gone. The federal government has tried to sell it three times and failed.

We want to keep the structure and make it impossible to ignore.

---

## The Idea

Cover every exterior surface in screens.

Not subtle lighting. Not tasteful accents. Thirteen terraces of LED display wrapping the entire building.

The Sphere in Las Vegas cost $2.3 billion and exists to sell concert tickets. We propose the same technology in service of education.

The building wouldn't house a broadcast studio. The building would BE the broadcast.

---

## What This Looks Like

**Dawn:** The building wakes up. Screens illuminate from the bottom terrace upward, one floor at a time.

**Morning:** Kelly appears. "Good morning, learners."

**Day:** Lessons play on the facade. A child in Tokyo watches the same lesson that's currently displayed on a building in California.

**Night:** The building glows. Not with preserved brutalist concrete. With whatever we want it to show.

Every learner on Earth can see where their lesson comes from.

---

## Why This Works

**1. The structure stays.**

The concrete isn't demolished. The stepped form remains. Pereira's engineering survives. We're adding a skin, not tearing down bones.

**2. The ugly disappears.**

The pale yellow concrete panels that everyone hates become invisible behind the display surface. The building transforms from eyesore to landmark.

**3. The purpose becomes visible.**

Most educational institutions hide behind walls. This one teaches by being seen. The mission isn't explained. It's displayed.

**4. The broadcast origin is literal.**

When Kelly says "Good morning" from the building, she's not in a studio inside the building. She's ON the building. The medium is the message.

---

## What This Costs

The Sphere's exterior: approximately $500 million for 580,000 square feet of LED.

This building's exterior surface: larger, but doesn't need the same resolution or curvature engineering.

Rough estimate: $200–400 million for the display infrastructure.

That's before fixing the actual building — the asbestos, the HVAC, the fire systems. Federal reports estimate $916 million in deferred maintenance.

Total realistic budget: $1–1.5 billion.

This is not a small project.

---

## Who Will Hate This

**Neighbors.** Light pollution. Visibility. "This isn't what we moved here for." They'll fight it at every city council meeting.

**Preservationists.** "You wrapped Pereira in pixels." The architecture community will have opinions.

**Dark sky advocates.** A building that glows is a building that pollutes the night.

**NIMBYs.** Every variety. They'll find reasons.

---

## Who Might Support This

**People who think the building is ugly.** Most people.

**People who want it to survive.** If someone doesn't do something interesting, it gets demolished for tract homes.

**People who believe education should be visible.** Not hidden in classrooms. Displayed at scale.

**People who like bold things.** This is a bold thing.

---

## The Honest Pitch

We're not preserving this building. We're not demolishing it. We're transforming it into something that didn't exist before: the world's largest educational display.

It will be controversial. It will be expensive. It will change what the building means.

But when it's done, every child who sees it will know that learning matters enough to put on a building.

---

## Current Status

**Owner:** U.S. General Services Administration (federal government)

**Status:** Three failed auction attempts. Legal disputes between bidders. GSA cancelled the most recent auction in May 2025 and said they would relaunch.

**Condition:** Classified "exceptionally high risk." Asbestos, fire safety issues, deferred maintenance since the 1980s.

**Zoning:** Federal/institutional. City of Laguna Niguel controls rezoning after sale. They have not committed to residential rezoning, which is why developers are nervous.

**Path to acquisition:** GSA can sell via auction, negotiate directly, or convey for public benefit. Educational use is a valid disposition pathway.
