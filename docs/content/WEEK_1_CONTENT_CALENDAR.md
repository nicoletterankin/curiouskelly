When you vividly imagine performing an action, your brain activates almost identically to when you actually do it.

Brain scans show ~90% overlap in motor cortex activity.

Your brain literally can't tell the difference.

—

Tweet 3:
In one famous study, people were divided into groups:

Group A: Physically practiced piano for 5 days
Group B: Only IMAGINED practicing
Group C: Did nothing

The results shocked researchers...

—

Tweet 4:
Groups A and B showed nearly identical brain changes.

Mental rehearsal created real neuroplastic changes.

The imaginary pianists got almost as good as the real ones.

—

Tweet 5:
This is why:

• Surgeons visualize procedures before operating
• Musicians rehearse performances in their minds
• Athletes "see" their perfect run before it happens

The key is specificity. Vague daydreaming doesn't work.

---

### 🐦 TWITTER THREAD

```
Tweet 1:
You will forget 70% of what you learned today.

Within 24 hours.

This isn't a flaw. It's a feature.

Your brain has a garbage collector. And it's very aggressive.

Here's how it decides what to keep 🧵

—

Tweet 2:
In 1885, Hermann Ebbinghaus memorized thousands of nonsense syllables to study memory.

What he discovered changed everything we know about learning:

The "Forgetting Curve"

—

Tweet 3:
Without review:
• After 20 min: 42% forgotten
• After 1 hour: 56% forgotten  
• After 24 hours: 70% forgotten
• After 1 week: 90% forgotten

Your brain assumes: If you don't use it, you don't need it.

—

Tweet 4:
But there's a hack.

Every time you recall something, you reset the forgetting curve.

And here's the key: the more times you recall it, the SLOWER you forget.

This is called the "Spacing Effect."

—

Tweet 5:
Instead of cramming:

• Learn something new
• Review it 1 day later
• Review it 3 days later
• Review it 1 week later
• Review it 2 weeks later

Each review takes less time, and the memory lasts longer.

—

Tweet 6:
This is why:

• Cramming feels productive but doesn't last
• Flashcard apps space out your reviews
• The best learners study LESS, more often

Your brain rewards consistency over intensity.

—

Tweet 7:
Quick test:

What was yesterday's lesson about?

If you remember, you just spaced your first review.

If you forgot... now you know why 🧠

Tomorrow: The power of sleep (your brain's secret study session)
```
