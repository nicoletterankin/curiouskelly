## Visual Assets

### Priority 1: Essential Animations & Demos

#### 1.1 Layer-by-Layer Build Animation
**Purpose:** Show how 3D printing works at a fundamental level
**Duration:** 30-45 seconds

**Specification:**
- Show a simple object (cube, pyramid, or small figurine) being built layer by layer
- Start with empty build platform
- Show nozzle/laser moving in X-Y pattern for each layer
- Platform lowers (FDM) or raises (SLA) incrementally
- Time-lapse but slow enough to understand the process
- Side view showing layers stacking up
- Optional: Split screen showing FDM vs. SLA vs. SLS simultaneously

**Age Adaptations:**
- Ages 2-5: Very simple shape (cube), bright colors, fun music
- Ages 6-12: Medium complexity (toy car or simple figure)
- Ages 13+: Complex geometry showing support structures and overhangs

**Kelly's Narration:**
- Ages 2-5: "Watch! The machine makes it layer by layer, like stacking pancakes!"
- Ages 6-12: "See how it builds from the bottom up? Each layer sticks to the one below it."
- Ages 13+: "Notice how the nozzle follows a precise path calculated by slicing software..."

---

#### 1.2 Technology Comparison Visual
**Purpose:** Show FDM vs. SLA vs. SLS side by side
**Format:** Static infographic or simple animation

**Content:**
| Technology | Visual | How It Works | Materials | Cost |
|------------|--------|--------------|-----------|------|
| **FDM** | Nozzle extruding plastic spaghetti | Melts plastic, extrudes layer by layer | PLA, ABS, PETG, TPU | $200-$5,000 |
| **SLA** | Laser curing liquid resin | UV light hardens liquid resin | Photopolymer resins | $300-$5,000 |
| **SLS** | Laser sintering powder | Laser fuses powder particles | Nylon, TPU powder | $5,000-$500,000 |

**Visual Elements:**
- Simple diagram of each printer type
- Material state (solid filament → liquid → powder)
- Light/heat source illustrated
- Finished part quality comparison

**Age Adaptations:**
- Ages 2-5: Skip this (too technical)
- Ages 6-12: Simple version showing just FDM (most relatable)
- Ages 13+: Full comparison chart

##  Interactive Components

### Activity 1: "What Would YOU Design?"
**Purpose:** Spark creativity and personal connection
**Format:** Drawing prompt or simple 3D design interface

**Prompt by Age:**

**Ages 2-5:**
"If you could make anything with a magic building machine, what would it be? Draw it!"
- Pre-drawn templates to color: toy, cup, helper tool
- Option to draw their own

**Ages 6-12:**
"Design something you would 3D print. Would you make:
- A toy or game piece?
- Something to help someone?
- A replacement part for something broken?
- Art or decoration?
- Something else?"

Draw or describe it. What would it look like? What colors?

**Ages 13+:**
"What problem in your life or community could be solved with 3D printing? Design a solution."
- Sketch it
- Describe materials needed
- Explain how it would work
- Who would benefit?

**Output:** Save/share designs, celebrate creativity

---

### Activity 2: "Should Companies Share Designs?" (Poll/Discussion)
**Purpose:** Engage critical thinking about IP and access
**Format:** Interactive poll with discussion prompts

**Question:**
"Should toy companies be required to share 3D design files for replacement parts, so kids can print new pieces instead of buying whole new toys?"

**Options:**
- ☑ Yes - reduces waste and saves families money
- ☐ No - companies should protect their designs
- ☐ Unsure - it's complicated

**Follow-Up (based on response):**

**If Yes:**
"You're thinking about environmental impact and fairness! But here's a challenge: If companies can't make money selling replacement products, how do they stay in business? What's a solution that's fair for everyone?"

**If No:**
"You're thinking about how businesses need to make money! But what about all the waste and the families who can't afford to replace entire toys? How could we balance business needs with environmental and social needs?"

**If Unsure:**
"You're right—it IS complicated! This is the kind of question our society has to figure out together. What information would help you decide?"

**Available for:** Ages 6+ (adapt language)

---

### Activity 3: "Explore a Makerspace" (Virtual or Local)
**Purpose:** Connect to real-world access points
**Format:** Interactive map or virtual tour

**Options:**

**Option A: Find Local Makerspaces**
- Enter zip code
- See nearby libraries, schools, or community centers with 3D printers
- Hours, cost (usually free), and how to access
- Link to Fab Lab network map

**Option B: Virtual Makerspace Tour**
- 360° photos or video walkthrough of a real makerspace
- Clickable hotspots: "This is a 3D printer," "This is where you learn CAD," "This is the community workspace"
- Meet real makers (short video intros)

**Available for:** Ages 6+

---

### Activity 4: "Repair or Replace?" (Decision Game)
**Purpose:** Practice systems thinking about consumption
**Format:** Interactive scenarios

**Scenarios (choose one per play):**

**Scenario 1: The Broken Toy**
"Your action figure's arm snapped off. The toy cost $15. What do you do?"
- A) Throw it away and ask for a new one ($15)
- B) Try to fix it with glue (free, might not work)
- C) Find a 3D printable file and print a new arm ($1, if you have access to printer)

**Reveal:** Show the environmental cost, money cost, and time cost of each option

**Scenario 2: The Coffee Machine**
"The drip tray on your coffee machine cracked. A new machine costs $80. What do you do?"
- A) Buy a new machine ($80, old one to landfill)
- B) Use it without the drip tray (free, but messy)
- C) Search for a 3D printable replacement part ($2 at makerspace)

**Scenario 3: The Phone Case**
"Your phone case cracked. What do you do?"
- A) Buy a new case ($25)
- B) Keep using the cracked one (free)
- C) Design and print a custom case ($3 at makerspace, exactly your style)

**After Each Choice:**
Show cumulative impact: "Over a year, this approach would save you $X and prevent Y items from going to the landfill."

**Available for:** Ages 6+

---

### Activity 5: "Design an Open-Source Solution" (Ages 13+)
**Purpose:** Engage in real maker thinking
**Format:** Guided design challenge

**Prompt:**
"Identify a problem in your school or community that could be solved with 3D printing. Design an open-source solution and decide whether to share it freely or sell it. Defend your choice."

**Steps:**
1. Identify the problem (What's broken? What's missing? Who's excluded?)
2. Sketch a solution
3. List materials needed
4. Estimate cost
5. **Critical Question:** Will you share your design openly (like e-NABLE) or keep it proprietary? Why?
6. What could go wrong? (Safety, legal, practical issues)

**Output:** Share designs in community gallery, vote on favorites

## Hands-On Activities

### For Those WITHOUT Printer Access

#### Activity A: "Layer-by-Layer Drawing"
**Ages:** 2-12
**Materials:** Paper, colored pencils/crayons

**Instructions:**
1. Draw the bottom layer of an object (like a snowman's bottom ball)
2. On a new sheet, draw the next layer up (middle ball)
3. On another sheet, draw the top layer (head)
4. Stack the drawings to see how 3D printing builds objects layer by layer

**Learning Goal:** Understand additive process without equipment

---

#### Activity B: "Playdough Printing"
**Ages:** 2-8
**Materials:** Playdough, plastic syringe or frosting pipper

**Instructions:**
1. Put playdough in syringe/pipper (like 3D printer "filament")
2. Squeeze out lines to make shapes on a plate (like a printer nozzle)
3. Build up layers to make a simple object
4. Observe how each layer sticks to the one below

**Learning Goal:** Physical understanding of extrusion and layer adhesion

---

#### Activity C: "Design in Tinkercad" (Free online tool)
**Ages:** 6+
**Materials:** Computer or tablet with internet

**Instructions:**
1. Go to Tinkercad.com (free, browser-based)
2. Follow beginner tutorial to make a simple object
3. Experiment with shapes, combinations, modifications
4. Download the design file (STL)
5. Optional: Find a local makerspace to print it

**Learning Goal:** Digital design skills, understanding the design-to-print pipeline

---

### For Those WITH Printer Access

#### Activity D: "Print and Share"
**Ages:** 6+
**Materials:** 3D printer access (home, school, library, makerspace)

**Instructions:**
1. Choose an e-NABLE prosthetic hand design OR a tool/toy from Thingiverse
2. Print it (with adult help if needed)
3. If prosthetic: donate to e-NABLE chapter
4. If tool/toy: gift it to someone who needs it
5. Share your experience

**Learning Goal:** Full workflow, open-source participation, community contribution

---

#### Activity E: "Fix Something"
**Ages:** 6+
**Materials:** 3D printer access, broken household item

**Instructions:**
1. Find something broken around your home (toy, appliance part, organizer, etc.)
2. Search Thingiverse/Printables for a replacement part OR
3. Measure the broken part and design a replacement in Tinkercad
4. Print it
5. Install it and test
6. Document the repair (photos, cost saved)

**Learning Goal:** Right to repair, practical problem-solving, cost savings

## Engagement Prompts by Age

### Ages 2-5

**Throughout Lesson:**
- "Can you make the building motion with your hands?"
- "What color would YOUR printed toy be?"
- "Should we help people who need special tools? Yeah!"

**Wonder Prompts:**
- "Wow! Look how big that printer is!"
- "That machine made a whole house!"
- "Emma can play better now because people shared!"

**Movement:**
- Stack hands up (like layers)
- Pretend to squeeze frosting (like extrusion)
- Build with blocks (physical additive process)

---

### Ages 6-12

**Throughout Lesson:**
- "Have you ever had something break and wished you could fix it?"
- "What would you design if you could make anything?"
- "How could 3D printing help your school or neighborhood?"

**Critical Thinking:**
- "Why do you think the printed hand costs $50 but the regular one costs $50,000?"
- "Is it better to make things nearby or far away? Why?"
- "Should we share designs or keep them secret?"

**Creative Prompts:**
- Draw your design
- Name three things you'd print
- Imagine a giant robot printer - what would it make?

---

### Ages 13-17

**Throughout Lesson:**
- "Who has power in this story?"
- "What would happen if everyone had access to 3D printers?"
- "Is this technology liberating or just creating new problems?"

**Critical Analysis:**
- "Why doesn't healthcare make prosthetics affordable if they could?"
- "What stops 3D printing from being truly democratic?"
- "How could this technology be misused?"

**Action Prompts:**
- "What would you design to help your community?"
- "Would you share your design openly or sell it? Defend your choice."
- "How can you participate in the maker movement, even without owning a printer?"

---

### Ages 18+

**Throughout Lesson:**
- "How would your industry change with distributed manufacturing?"
- "What policy changes would support democratization?"
- "What are the hidden costs we're not discussing?"

**Systems Thinking:**
- "Analyze the economic model: Who profits? Who benefits? Who's excluded?"
- "What infrastructure is required for true democratization?"
- "How do we balance innovation, safety, and access?"

**Strategic Questions:**
- "How could you bring 3D printing access to your community?"
- "What skills would you need to teach or learn?"
- "What's one concrete action you'll take this month?"

## Kelly's Avatar & Voice Direction

### Personality & Energy

**Core Traits:**
- Curious and excited (not lecturing, discovering together)
- Thoughtful about complexity (acknowledges tensions, doesn't oversimplify)
- Empowering ("You can participate in this!")
- Authentic (shares her own learning and questions)

---

### Age-Specific Kelly

**Ages 2-5 (Kelly Age: 3, Playful-Toddler):**
- Voice: Bright, warm, lots of expression
- Language: Simple, sensory, wonder-filled
- Energy: High, playful, celebratory

- Energy: Passionate, earnest about justice
- Examples: "This is complicated..." "What bothers me is..." "You have the power to..."

**Ages 18-35 (Kelly Age: 27, Knowledgeable-Adult):**

**Ages 61-102 (Kelly Age: 82, Reflective-Elder):**

### Accessibility Considerations

**Visual:**
- All images have alt-text descriptions
- Color is not the only indicator (use icons + color)
- High contrast mode available
- Text size adjustable

**Audio:**
- All Kelly narration has text captions/subtitles
- Visual animations have audio descriptions
- Sound is optional, not required

**Interaction:**
- Keyboard navigation for all interactive elements
- Touch targets large enough (44x44px minimum)
- Clear focus indicators
- Instructions available in multiple formats

---

### Multilingual Assets

**Priority:**
- Kelly's voiceover: EN, ES, FR
- All text overlays: EN, ES, FR
- Interactive prompts: EN, ES, FR

**Visual Assets:**
- Most photos/animations are language-agnostic
- Infographics need translated versions (text overlay)
- Charts and labels need translation

---

### Technical Specifications

**Video/Animation:**
- Format: MP4 (H.264)
- Resolution: 1920x1080 minimum
- Frame rate: 30fps minimum
- Length: 30-90 seconds per animation

**Images:**
- Format: JPG (photos), PNG (graphics with transparency)
- Resolution: 1920x1080 minimum for full-screen, 800x600 for thumbnails
- Optimization: Compress for web without losing quality

**Interactive Elements:**
- HTML5/JavaScript (web-compatible)
- Responsive design (works on tablets and phones)
- Touch and click both supported
